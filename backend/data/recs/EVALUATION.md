# How well the recommender works

Generated by `scripts/recs/evaluate.js --final` on 2026-10-20 · MovieLens ml-32m (ratings Jan 1995 – Oct 2023) · 826 of 855 catalogue films found in MovieLens · 7,110 test people (group 2, never used to choose anything) · Node v20.20.2

## In one line each

- **Next film:** given the last 5 films a person loved, EASE put the very next one in its top 10 for **42.1%** of people (old engine 12.1%, 3.5×; first engine 35.3%; the most popular films 27.6%).
- **Any later love:** for **72.2%** of people, at least one film they went on to love was in the top 10, from just their last 5 loves (old engine 32.6%; most popular 57.2%).
- **Held-out loves (published protocol):** Recall@20 **69.2%** (most popular 50.3%; random 2.5%).

Multiples (×) are point estimates; the ± margins below are the measured uncertainty.

## How it was tested

- People are split by id: 80% build the counts, 10% choose the settings (validation), 10% are kept for this report (test). Nobody is scored on films they helped count.
- Three lenses on the same 7,110 people (everyone who loved at least 5 of our films). They are views of one test, not independent confirmations.
  - **Next film** (strict): rewind each person to just before the last film they loved; ratings after that moment never happened. A hit is that film in the top 10. Two settings, as on the site: For You (their last 5 loves) and Double Bill (1 film).
  - **Any later love** (time-ordered): rewind to when they had loved 80% of their films; every film they loved afterwards is a target. Input: their last 5 loves before that moment.
  - **Held-out loves** (Liang et al. 2018; Steck 2019): a fixed random 20% of their loves is held out; the other 80% is the input. Recall@k is Liang et al.'s: hits over the smaller of k and the number held out.
- Every method ranks every candidate film. We never rank against a sample of 100 random films: sampled metrics inflate scores and can even reorder methods (Krichene & Rendle 2020).
- The engine was chosen on validation, by a rule fixed beforehand: Among settings within one paired standard error of the best validation NDCG@10 (next film, For You), the one with the highest catalogue coverage@10, then the smaller λ, β and γ. Chosen: EASE, λ = 100, β = 0 (content), γ = 0 (canon nudge); each film keeps its 50 strongest positive weights.
- The evaluation code was committed before this run (commit `cab0954`, SHA-256 of the code `d7dd5099057fdc7f…`), so the test was fixed before its numbers existed.
- ± is a 95% interval for sampling noise across people. "EASE minus" rows compare on the same people. The primary comparison is EASE minus re-tuned item-kNN on next-film NDCG@10 (For You); everything else is secondary.

## Limits

- MovieLens people are not this site's visitors; the numbers say how well the engine predicts film lovers in general.
- Other people's ratings made after a test person's moment still feed the counts. That favours the methods built on counts (item-kNN, EASE) over content, canon order and the old engine, which use no ratings; it is standard for this kind of test.
- MovieLens timestamps record when a rating was entered, not when the film was watched: "next film" often means "next film rated in a sign-up session".
- Held-out loves is the generous lens: the split ignores time, so the input can include films loved after the held-out ones; and with 826 films rather than ~20,000, even random picks score Recall@20 ≈ 2.5%. Its numbers are not comparable to published results on MovieLens-20M. It is also the lens closest to the site, which uses every film you've touched.
- 29 catalogue films aren't in MovieLens (mostly 2023 and later) and 5 more had no loves in the training group. They get neighbours from content alone, and other films point to them through a weaker content link — a product choice that can't be tested here. When a short list runs out, the rest of a top 100 is filled in canon order.
- On the site, a favourite or a 4–5 star rating counts fully and a watched or watchlisted film counts half. Those weights are a product choice; MovieLens has no equivalent to test them on.
- The shipped model is refitted on everyone (train + validation + test) with the same settings, so it has slightly more data than the one tested here.
