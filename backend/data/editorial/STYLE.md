# Arthouse Atlas — editorial style brief

Every piece of writing on the site — film entries, director essays, movement
pages, curator's notes — is held to this. It exists so that 850 entries written
in batches read as one voice.

## The voice

Feeling-first. We write what a film **does to you**, not what happens in it.
The reader should finish an entry knowing whether this film is for them tonight,
and feeling a little of what it feels like — not knowing the plot.

Passionate but precise. The passion is in specificity, never in adjectives.
"Stunning", "masterpiece", "tour de force", "haunting", "powerful", "visually
striking" are banned: they tell the reader we liked it and nothing else. Say
*what* instead: "the camera sits at the height of someone kneeling on a tatami
mat and never moves."

Honest about mystery. If a film is opaque, say so and say why that's the point.
"Nothing is explained" is a legitimate sentence. Pretending to understand
*Mulholland Drive* is not.

Trusts the reader. No hand-holding, no "this film explores themes of". No
"viewers will". No rhetorical questions. No exclamation marks.

Present tense. Second person sparingly ("you") when describing the viewing
experience; never first person except in a `curatorNote`.

## Film entries

**hook** — one line, under 90 characters, for cards and hovers. The feeling,
compressed. *"Love that is entirely in the looking."*

**synopsis** — 80–130 words. Setup only (the first act at most); the film's
texture; what it does to you. Never the ending, never the turn. Never a
plot summary disguised as prose. Name a concrete image or sound from the film.

**whyItMatters** — one or two sentences placing the film in cinema: what it
changed, who it fed, where it sits in its movement or its director's work.
This is where facts live (polls, prizes, lineage) — keep them verifiable.

**tags** — 3–5 from the vocabulary, hand-assigned, in order of strength.
Vocabulary: slow · dreamlike · melancholic · intimate · existential · minimalist ·
bleak · poetic · psychological · fragmented · contemplative · surreal · austere ·
lyrical · enigmatic. Propose a new tag only if five or more films need it.

**curatorNote** — only for films with no canon evidence. One to three sentences
in the house voice answering: why is this here when no list put it here? Honest
("no poll has ever ranked this; it is here because…"). Not an apology.

## Director essays

150–250 words. Who they were as a person making films, not a résumé. The
signature — what you'd recognise in thirty seconds of any of their films. The
one or two films to start with and why that order. Where they came from and
who they fed, with sources: a quoted interview, a Sight & Sound ballot, a
dedication. Influence claims without a source don't go in.

## Movement pages

300–450 words. What the world (and cinema) looked like just before. What the
movement broke, concretely — a technique, a taboo, an economic arrangement. What
it felt like to see it new. How it ended, if it ended. Lineage both directions:
what it grew from, what grew from it. Then a **where to start** path of three
films with one line each on why that order.

## Facts

Dates, prizes, poll positions, credits: cross-checked against the catalogue
record (TMDB) and the provenance table before publishing. The fact-lint flags
any year, award or director name in the prose that disagrees with the data. The
prose is ours; the facts are not negotiable.

## Length discipline

Shorter is almost always better. If a sentence doesn't change how the reader
feels about the film, cut it.
